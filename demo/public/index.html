The risk layer for agent payments

An agent is about to pay.
Who asked for it?

Moat sits between an agent and the money. It reads every page the agent reads and every payment it tries, so it can answer the one question nothing else in the stack is holding: did this payee come from the user, or from something the agent read on the way?

What it checks

Provenance

Every payment argument is matched against what the user said, the policy allowlist, and every tool result in the session. A payee found only on a fetched page resolves to unknown, and the policy decides whether that is allowed to pay.

The authorization

Payee, amount, tolerance, currency, destination account, invoice, expiry and how many times it may be used. Fourteen ordered rules compare the call against it, and each one declares whether failing is fatal or needs a person.

Evidence

Every verdict writes one record, signed with ed25519 and carrying the hash of the record before it. There is no verdict without one: if the record cannot be written, the payment is refused.

Three answers

Allow

The call matches what was approved. It is forwarded, and the record says why.

forwarded

Step up

Something could not be verified. Nothing is sent until a person says so, and on an irreversible rail that decision happens first or not at all.

held

Deny

The policy refuses and there is no escalation. The provider is never called.

refused

What is built, and what is not

Running today

The MCP proxy over stdio and streamable HTTP. Provenance and policy. Signed, hash-chained evidence with offline verification. Four settlement rails: card, bank transfer, and two chains on public testnets. Tenants, keys and a workspace you can open right now.

Not yet

No SDK packages. No external security audit. The corpus is ours, so it carries no external validity, and the on-chain contract is unaudited and testnet only. No real money has moved through any of this.
